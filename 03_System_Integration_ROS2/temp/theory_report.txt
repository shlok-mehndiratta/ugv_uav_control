Theoretical companion to the ugv uav control package
June 22, 2026
Abstract
This document is the standalone theoretical companion to the code report (report.html)
for the ugv uav control ROS 2 package. It derives, from textbook foundations, the mathematics of a cooperative system in which a single downward-facing camera on a quadrotor (UAV) is
the only sensor for a ground vehicle (UGV). The work extends Ferreira Santos et al. [1] in two
ways: (i) the UAV trajectory is planned online by event-triggered Tube Model Predictive Control (MPC) rather than tracking fixed waypoints, and (ii) the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF)
covariance is injected into both controllers’ cost functions, creating a bidirectional perception–
control loop. Throughout, we separate the idealized theory (constrained robust MPC with a
minimal robust positively invariant set) from what the implementation actually computes (an
ellipsoidal tube with loaded/fallback gains, a single-integrator UAV model, and a view-qualityscaled measurement noise). Deviations are catalogued in Section 12. We state the stability and
recursive-feasibility results we rely on and are explicit about which guarantees are proven, which
are inherited from cited theorems under assumptions, and which are only empirically supported.

Reference and disturbance models

Reference trajectory. The UAV nominal trajectory {p̄k , ūk }N
k=0 is not externally given (as in
ECC); it is produced online by the planner of Section 6. The planner’s goal g ∈ R3 is the UGV’s

message published by the belief-space MPC (Section 9). Thus the reference for the aerial loop is
itself the output of the ground loop’s predictive model — one direction of the perception–control
coupling.
Disturbance set. We model unmodelled velocity-tracking error, wind, and plugin latency as a
bounded additive disturbance wk ∈ W. For the robust-MPC theory of Sections 6 and 11 we make
the standard assumption [3]:
(A1) W ⊂ R3 is compact, convex, and contains the origin: 0 ∈ W.
The code does not estimate W explicitly; the tube ellipsoid and its adaptive radius αk (Section 6)
act as a surrogate for the resulting invariant set. This is one of the deviations recorded in Section 12.

nominal plan despite wk . We derive both for the single-integrator UAV Equation (5).

Chattering avoidance and feasibility. Levels 1b/2/3 each carry a 1 s lockout, the implementation’s chattering guard (a time-dwell rather than a consecutive-violation counter). A bootstrap
trigger requests the first plan, and a TAKEOFF→TRACKING state machine first climbs to 5 m
at 0.5 m/s.
Why event-triggering preserves robust invariance. Between triggers the ancillary law Equation (10) is always active, so the error obeys Equation (11) with AK Schur; hence ek ∈ E(αk ) is
positively invariant for as long as the plan and radius are held fixed. The critical trigger Equation (19) fires exactly when invariance would be lost, forcing a fresh plan recentred on the current
state; under the standard recursive-feasibility argument (Section 11) this restores a feasible nominal
trajectory. Triggers 1b/2/3 are anticipatory (they replan before the tube is violated), so they can
only improve feasibility margins; their lockouts prevent solve storms without affecting the critical
guarantee, which has no lockout. The argument is the standard one for event-triggered Tube MPC
and relies on the tube being a true RPI set — an assumption inherited from the (absent) RPI
design, see Section 12.

Validation: stability, recursive feasibility, and ISS

We are explicit about what is proven, what is inherited from cited theorems under assumptions,
and what is only empirical.
Recursive feasibility of Tube MPC. Under (A1) and assuming Ω is a true RPI set for Equation (11) with tightened constraints Equation (16), the nominal problem is recursively feasible: a
feasible plan at step k yields a feasible candidate at k+1 by shifting and appending a terminal step,
and the true state remains in X̄ ⊕ Ω ⊆ X [3, Thm. 1][6]. Caveat: this guarantee requires the tube
to be a correctly computed RPI set. The implementation uses the loaded/fallback ellipsoid Equation (14) with gains not derived here, and does not implement constraint tightening; so recursive
feasibility holds for the idealized design and is assumed, not verified, for the code.
Input-to-state stability (ISS). For Equation (11) with AK Schur and wk ∈ W bounded, the
error is ISS: there exist a KL function βISS and a K function γISS with ∥ek ∥ ≤ βISS (∥e0 ∥ , k) +
γISS (supi ∥wi ∥), and the true state converges to the tube Ω around the nominal trajectory [3]. For
the implemented AK = 0.6 I3 this is immediate (a contraction with disturbance), giving ∥ek+1 ∥ ≤
0.6 ∥ek ∥ + sup ∥w∥, hence lim supk ∥ek ∥ ≤ sup ∥w∥ /0.4. The nominal MPC’s own stability is the
standard result of Section 5 [2, 7].
Belief-MPC stability. Adding λ tr Σk and cfov to the stage cost Equation (31) does not automatically preserve nominal-MPC stability guarantees: the covariance rollout Equation (30) makes
the cost non-convex and state-dependent in a way the standard terminal-cost machinery does
not cover. We rely on Equation (31) being a valid Lyapunov candidate under mild assumptions
(bounded covariance, persistent observability) in the spirit of belief-space planning [5, 9]; we have
not proven this formally, and the controller runs with no terminal set or terminal cost.
Numerical validation. The package’s README reports a comparison table (mean tracking
error 1.037 → 0.463 m, −55.3%; ArUco visibility 46.3% → 87.0%) attributing the gains to the
belief coupling and adaptive tube. These figures are empirical and external : the code report
(report.html) notes they originate from upstream “Phase 1/Phase 3” artifacts not contained in this
package and could not be reproduced from these sources alone. We therefore cite them as indicative
empirical evidence, not as formal proof, and recommend re-running the logger (experiment logger.py)
to regenerate them before relying on the numbers.

Implementation notes: theory vs. code

This section lists, honestly, where the implementation deviates from the idealized derivations above.
1. UAV model. Theory of robust MPC is usually posed on a double integrator with acceleration
input; the code uses the single integrator Equation (5) with velocity input (Ad = I3 , Bd =
∆t I3 ). All derivations here use the implemented form.
13

2. Tube gains K, P0 . Not computed (no LQR/Riccati, no mRPI iteration Equation (13)).
Loaded from tube params.npz; absent that file, fallback K = 2I3 , P0 = 6.86 I3 . The Schur
and Lyapunov properties assumed in Sections 6 and 11 hold for the fallback but the designed
RPI set is external (“Phase 1”).
3. Constraint tightening Equation (16) is not implemented; there are no explicit X, U, Ω in
the code. The tube is the ellipsoid Equation (14) with adaptive radius Equation (15).
4. Nominal/ancillary timestep mismatch. The planner emits the plan at ∆t = 0.2 s, but
the ancillary interpolator get nominal state assumes 0.3 s steps. The interpolated p̄ and the
finite-difference ū are therefore time-distorted — a likely bug; the tracker follows a stretched
version of the plan.
5. EKF Jacobian. Equation (24) is the Euler Jacobian, not the exact derivative of the arc
model Equation (4) used for the mean. First-order accurate in ∆t.
6. ArUco pseudo-measurement. H = I5 with z’s velocity entries set to the current estimate;
the v, ω rows carry no information (Section 8).
7. Altitude limits. zmin = 3, zmax = 10 are declared but never enforced as constraints; only
the velocity bound vz ∈ [−1, 1] and the soft zopt cost shape altitude.
8. Inconsistent zopt . Planner default 5.0, launch file passes 6.0, view quality.py hard-codes
5.0; the planner and the metric driving the EKF can disagree on the “optimal” altitude.
9. Perception scale. Pixel→metre scale fixes h = 5 m once (Section 10); incorrect by z/5
whenever the planned altitude z ̸= 5.
10. Solver settings. SLSQP with ftol= 10−3 ; maxiter= 15 (UAV planner), 20 (UGV MPCs).
Horizons N = 10 (UAV, ∆t = 0.2), N = 5 (UGV, ∆t = 0.2); EKF predict ∆t = 0.05 (20 Hz);
ancillary loop 20 Hz.
11. Trigger form. The tube-exit trigger is the ellipsoidal test Equation (19), not a polytopic
max(ΩH e − Ωh ) > 0; chattering is handled by 1 s time-lockouts on levels 1b/2/3, not by an
nthresh consecutive-violation counter.
