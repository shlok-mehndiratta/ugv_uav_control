\begin{abstract}
We present a genuinely-controlled, reproducible software-in-the-loop (SITL) study of
aerial--ground cooperative tracking in which a single downward UAV camera is the ground
vehicle's \emph{sole} exteroceptive sensor, serving simultaneously as its navigation
reference (path segmentation) and its state estimator (ArUco/EKF). We show this dual role
induces a fundamental altitude tension: low altitude sharpens estimation (ground-sampling
noise $R\propto z^2$) but starves navigation coverage (the path lookahead leaves the camera
footprint), while high altitude does the reverse. Our contributions are: (i) we formalize the
coverage--resolution tension and derive the perception-constrained altitude policy --- fly as
low as segmentation allows, climbing anticipatorily into turns; (ii) we characterize the
resulting cost tradeoff in reproducible SITL; and (iii) we show a perception-driven event
trigger is the computational enabler of the heavier belief-space planner, cutting online
planner solves $\approx\!\RESsolveRed\times$ ($\RESsolvesCone\!\to\!\RESsolvesCthree$ per run)
and total planner CPU $\approx\!\REScpuRed\times$ at matched estimation quality (covariance
$\REScov$, $100\%$ visibility for both continuous and event-triggered planning), trading a
small amount of trajectory smoothness for the compute saving. We are explicit about scope: the
isolated observability benefit is \emph{not} separable on this platform because the shared
camera couples navigation and estimation, and the low-altitude/high-speed corner is
non-reproducibly unstable --- both stated as findings. We also report a methodological point:
headless execution is a prerequisite for reproducible closed-loop active-perception study
(identical-configuration trajectories fork by $\approx\!\RESforkGUI$\,m with the Gazebo GUI
versus $\approx\!\RESforkHL$\,m headless).
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}

The deployment of Unmanned Ground Vehicles (UGVs) in unstructured and GPS-denied
environments is heavily constrained by their limited perception horizons.
Ground-level sensors such as LiDAR and stereo cameras suffer from occlusion,
narrow fields of view (FoV), and an inability to anticipate upcoming path
curvature. To mitigate this, aerial--ground cooperative systems employ an
Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) as an elevated ``eye in the sky'' for a
perception-limited UGV.

Most cooperative-tracking frameworks treat the UAV as a \emph{passive} observer:
it follows a predefined trajectory or simply minimizes distance to the UGV,
treating the UGV's state estimation as a downstream byproduct
\cite{mayne2000constrained}. This decoupling is fragile: when the UGV maneuvers
aggressively or is occluded, the UAV can lose the target, the estimation
covariance grows, and tracking degrades. Active-perception planning, where the
sensor trajectory is optimized to maximize information gain
\cite{falanga2018pampc}, addresses part of this but typically relies on
continuous, computationally expensive replanning and rarely integrates robust
tracking guarantees for the aerial vehicle.

This paper studies a \emph{bidirectional} observability-aware architecture in
which \emph{both} vehicles adapt to the ground vehicle's estimation uncertainty.
Its contributions, as realized in the accompanying open implementation, are:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Formalization of the single-camera coverage--resolution tension} and the
perception-constrained altitude policy: with one downward camera serving both the ground
vehicle's navigation (path segmentation) and its state estimate (ArUco/EKF)
\cite{platt2010belief,vandenberg2012motion}, low altitude sharpens estimation ($R\propto z^2$)
but starves navigation coverage; the optimum flies as low as segmentation allows, climbing
anticipatorily into turns.

\item \textbf{A genuinely-controlled, reproducible SITL realization} in ROS\,2/Gazebo
(vision/ArUco pipeline, view-quality-coupled EKF, belief-space UGV MPC, and the UAV
planner/ancillary pair \cite{mayne2005robust,eqtami2010event}), including a methodological
finding: headless execution is a prerequisite for reproducible closed-loop active-perception
study ($\approx\!\RESforkGUI$\,m vs.\ $\approx\!\RESforkHL$\,m identical-configuration
trajectory fork).

\item \textbf{A validated co-design result}: the perception-driven event trigger is the
computational enabler of the heavier belief-space planner, cutting online planner solves
$\approx\!\RESsolveRed\times$ and planner CPU $\approx\!\REScpuRed\times$ at matched estimation
quality, for a small loss of inter-trigger trajectory smoothness.
\end{itemize}
We are explicit about scope: the \emph{isolated} observability benefit is not separable on this
platform because the shared camera couples navigation and estimation, and the binding
low-altitude/high-speed corner is non-reproducibly unstable --- both stated as findings
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results}), not hidden.

We deliberately separate the idealized robust-MPC theory (offline mRPI, tightened
constraints, terminal-set recursive feasibility) from what the code enforces
online, and we validate the computational claim in simulation. The remainder of
the paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related
work; Section~\ref{sec:problem} states the problem; Section~\ref{sec:arch} gives
the vehicle and perception models; Section~\ref{sec:method} develops the coupled
event-triggered tube-MPC; Section~\ref{sec:theory} states the theoretical
properties and the exact implementation status; and Section~\ref{sec:results}
reports the simulation study.

\section{RELATED WORK}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Robust and tube MPC.} Tube MPC \cite{mayne2005robust} decomposes control
into a nominal plan and an ancillary law that confines the true state to a robust
positively invariant (RPI) tube; Rakovi\'c et al. \cite{mRPI} give the standard
outer approximation of the minimal RPI set by a truncated Minkowski series. These
guarantees assume a fixed, bounded disturbance set and constraint tightening
computed offline. Time-varying (homothetic/parameterized) tubes generalize the
fixed tube but require the scaling to enter the optimization with its own
feasibility argument; our online adaptivity (Section~\ref{sec:method}) is a
lighter-weight surrogate and we mark the homothetic treatment as future work.

\textbf{Perception-aware and active planning.} PAMPC \cite{falanga2018pampc}
optimizes quadrotor trajectories for perception objectives. Belief-space planning
\cite{platt2010belief,vandenberg2012motion} augments the planning state with the
estimator covariance. Our belief-space UGV controller is in this family. The
\emph{bidirectional} coupling --- the \emph{target} shaping its trajectory to stay
observable while the \emph{tracker} adapts its robustness to perception quality ---
is the design novelty of the architecture. What we \emph{evidence} in this paper is
narrower and concrete: the integrated SITL realization of that architecture and the
perception-driven trigger's computational result (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}).
Isolating the coupling's observability benefit is future work requiring the ground
vehicle's navigation to be decoupled from the tracking camera (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}).

\textbf{Event-triggered MPC.} Event-triggered schemes \cite{eqtami2010event}
re-solve only when a state-dependent condition fires. We instantiate a
perception-driven trigger with a critical tube-exit condition plus anticipatory
conditions.

\section{PROBLEM FORMULATION}
\label{sec:problem}

A UAV tracks a UGV in a global frame $\mathcal{F}_W$. The UAV carries the only
exteroceptive sensor: a downward monocular camera that detects an ArUco marker on
the UGV, yielding a noisy pose measurement whose quality depends on the imaging
geometry. An EKF fuses these detections with UGV wheel odometry to produce a
belief $(\mu,\Sigma)$ over the UGV state. The objective is to (i) keep the UGV
inside the camera footprint so that $\Sigma$ stays bounded, (ii) track the UGV
robustly under bounded disturbances, and (iii) do so while limiting the number of
online optimizations. The UGV must track a ground lane while remaining
observable.

\subsection{UGV dynamics}
The UGV uses unicycle kinematics with first-order actuator lag. Let
$\mathbf{x}_g = [x_g, y_g, \theta, v, \omega]^T$ be the state, $(x_g,y_g)$ the
position, $\theta$ the heading, $(v,\omega)$ the linear/angular velocities, and
$\mathbf{u}_g = [v_c, \omega_c]^T$ the commanded velocities. The discrete-time
dynamics are
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_{g,k+1} = \mathbf{f}_g(\mathbf{x}_{g,k}, \mathbf{u}_{g,k}) + \mathbf{w}_g,
\end{equation}
where the actuator lag is governed by time constants $\tau_v,\tau_\omega$ and
$\mathbf{w}_g$ is process noise.

\subsection{UAV model}
Although the platform is a quadrotor, the planner and the ancillary tracker treat
it as a \textbf{velocity-controlled point mass} (single integrator); an
inner-loop attitude/thrust controller, provided by the Gazebo velocity-control
plugin, is assumed to realize commanded velocities. With position
$\mathbf{p}=[p_x,p_y,p_z]^T$ and velocity command $\mathbf{u}=[v_x,v_y,v_z]^T$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}_{k+1} = \mathbf{p}_k + \Delta t\, \mathbf{u}_k + \mathbf{w}_k,
\qquad A_d=I_3,\ B_d=\Delta t\, I_3,
\label{eq:uav_disc}
\end{equation}
with bounded disturbance $\mathbf{w}_k\in\mathbb{W}$. All three axes are fully
actuated: $v_z$ is a genuine decision variable and the altitude policy of
Sec.~\ref{sec:coverage} is realized closed-loop in SITL (the UAV altitude spans
$[2.5,7]$\,m; Sec.~\ref{sec:results}). A
second-order double-integrator (acceleration input) is the more faithful
rigid-body abstraction; we use \eqref{eq:uav_disc} because it is exactly what the
implementation optimizes.

\subsection{View-quality-dependent perception}
The camera's vantage point affects estimation accuracy. We use a differentiable
view-quality metric $q_k\in(0,1]$ that degrades as the UGV nears the FoV edge and
as the altitude departs from a precision-optimal value $z_{\mathrm{opt}}$ (too low
loses footprint; too high loses pixel resolution). With horizontal UAV--UGV
distance $d=\lVert\mathbf{p}^{xy}-\mathbf{p}_g\rVert$ and footprint radius
$r_{\mathrm{fov}}(z)=z\tan(\phi/2)$,
\begin{equation}
q_k =
\underbrace{\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta(d-r_{\mathrm{fov}})}}}_{\text{FoV gate}}\!\cdot\!
\underbrace{e^{-d^2/(2\sigma_{xy}^2)}}_{\text{centering}}\!\cdot\!
\underbrace{e^{-(z-z_{\mathrm{opt}})^2/(2\sigma_z^2)}}_{\text{altitude precision}},
\label{eq:q}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_{xy}=r_{\mathrm{fov}}/2$. The altitude factor makes $q_k$
\emph{non-monotone} in $z$ (it does not tend to $1$ as $z\to\infty$), removing the
incentive to climb without bound.

The measurement covariance grows as view quality drops. The implementation scales
the baseline ArUco covariance $R_{\mathrm{full}}$ inversely with $q_k$,
\begin{equation}
R_k(q_k) = \frac{R_{\mathrm{full}}}{q_k + \epsilon},
\qquad \epsilon=10^{-3},
\label{eq:Rk}
\end{equation}
so $R_k\to R_{\mathrm{full}}$ for an excellent view and $R_k\to\infty$ (the
update ignores the marker) as $q_k\to0$. This couples UAV geometry directly to
EKF performance.

For the quantitative study we use the physically-grounded \emph{ground-sampling-distance}
(GSD) noise model: pixel-level localization is fixed while the GSD (m/px) grows linearly with
altitude, so the metric position-measurement covariance grows \emph{quadratically},
\begin{equation}
R(z) = R_{\mathrm{full}}\,(z/z_0)^2\,\big(1+(d/r_{\mathrm{fov}})^2\big),
\label{eq:Rgsd}
\end{equation}
with a mild FoV-edge inflation $(1+(d/r_{\mathrm{fov}})^2)$. Unlike the Gaussian-bowl form
\eqref{eq:Rk} --- which is non-monotone in $z$ and instead models resolution dropout away from a
nominal altitude --- \eqref{eq:Rgsd} is monotone increasing in $z$, matching the theory's
resolution assumption and the $R\propto z^2$ side of the coverage--resolution tension
(Sec.~\ref{sec:coverage}). Equation~\eqref{eq:Rgsd} is the measurement-noise model used in all
reported results.

\subsection{Segmentation coverage model}
\label{sec:coverage}
The same downward camera also supplies the UGV's \emph{navigation} reference: an OpenCV
segmenter extracts the lane and seeds a waypoint a lookahead distance ahead of the vehicle.
That waypoint must lie inside the camera footprint or segmentation fails and the UGV loses its
reference. With footprint radius $r_{\mathrm{fov}}(z)=z\tan(\phi/2)$ and required lookahead
$d_{\mathrm{look}}(\kappa)$ --- which grows with local path curvature $\kappa$, since a curving
lane exits the footprint sooner and needs more forward view --- define the non-dimensional
\emph{coverage factor}
\begin{equation}
\eta \;=\; \frac{r_{\mathrm{fov}}(z)}{d_{\mathrm{look}}(\kappa)}
      \;=\; \frac{z\tan(\phi/2)}{d_{\mathrm{look}}(\kappa)} ,
\label{eq:eta}
\end{equation}
with segmentation feasible for $\eta\gtrsim1$. This is the crux coupling: the estimator wants \emph{low} altitude ($R\propto z^2$, better
resolution), while \eqref{eq:eta} demands \emph{sufficient} altitude for navigation. The
estimation-optimal altitude is thus the lowest that keeps $\eta\gtrsim1$, and this floor rises
with curvature --- the analytic basis for climbing into turns. The aerial planner
(Sec.~\ref{sec:aerial}) enforces $\eta\gtrsim1$ as a soft lower bound; Sec.~\ref{sec:results}
reports the empirical cost tradeoff over $\eta$.

\section{Coupled Event-Triggered Tube MPC}
\label{sec:method}

The estimation uncertainty $\Sigma_k$ enters the control formulations of both
vehicles.

\subsection{Information-aware UGV control (belief-space NMPC)}
The UGV controller augments the planning state with the covariance,
$\xi_k=(\mu_k,\Sigma_k)$, and \emph{propagates both through the horizon}. At each
predicted step it forms the no-observation prediction
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{pred}}=F\Sigma F^T+Q$ and a post-measurement covariance
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}$ (Joseph update), and blends
them by a smooth visibility gate
$\gamma=1/(1+e^{\beta(d-r_{\mathrm{fov}})})$:
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_k = (1-\gamma)\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{pred}} + \gamma\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}.
\label{eq:blend}
\end{equation}
The cost minimizes tracking error while penalizing predicted uncertainty and FoV
exit,
\begin{equation}
J_g = \sum_{k=0}^{N_g-1}\!\Big(
\lVert\mathbf{e}_{g,k}\rVert_Q^2
+\lVert\mathbf{u}_{g,k}\rVert_R^2
+\lambda\,\tr(\Sigma_k)
+ w_{\mathrm{fov}}\,c_{\mathrm{fov}}(\mathbf{x}_{g,k})\Big),
\label{eq:Jg}
\end{equation}
where the FoV term is a one-sided quadratic hinge
$c_{\mathrm{fov}}=\big(\max(0,\,d_k-r_{\mathrm{fov}})\big)^2$ (a soft constraint,
not an asymptotic barrier). Crucially, the belief rollout \eqref{eq:blend} uses the
\emph{same} measurement-noise model as the on-board EKF --- the GSD model \eqref{eq:Rgsd} in
all reported runs (both share one implementation, selected by a single flag) --- so the planner
propagates the estimator's actual uncertainty, not a surrogate. The covariance rollout
\eqref{eq:blend} makes \eqref{eq:Jg} a nonconvex NLP, solved with SLSQP over $N_g=5$. The two
terms $\lambda\tr(\Sigma_k)$ and $w_{\mathrm{fov}}c_{\mathrm{fov}}$ are what make the
controller belief-coupled and are absent from the passive baseline.

\subsection{Aerial nominal planner and altitude policy}
\label{sec:aerial}
The UAV computes a nominal trajectory $\bar{\mathbf{p}}$ over $N_a=10$ by
optimizing goal tracking, an altitude regularizer toward $z_{\mathrm{opt}}$, and a
visibility term that tolerates lateral offset only when altitude is sufficient:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
J_a = \sum_{k}w_{\ell,k}\Big[\,
&\lVert\bar{\mathbf{p}}_k-\mathbf{g}\rVert^2
+ w_z(z_k-z_{\mathrm{opt}})^2 \\[-1pt]
&+ w_{\mathrm{alt}}\frac{\lVert\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{xy}_k-\mathbf{g}^{xy}\rVert^2}{z_k^2+0.1}
\,\Big] + w_u\lVert\bar{\mathbf{u}}_k\rVert^2,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Ja}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{g}$ is the UGV's predicted end-of-horizon position (read from the
belief-MPC rollout, one direction of the coupling) and $w_{\ell,k}$ up-weights
later steps. The perception-constrained altitude policy of Sec.~\ref{sec:coverage} sets the
altitude setpoint explicitly to the coverage floor plus an anticipatory margin,
\begin{equation}
z_k \;=\; \hat d_k/\tan(\phi/2) \;+\; \beta_\kappa\,\hat\kappa_k \;+\; z_{\mathrm{m}},
\label{eq:altpol}
\end{equation}
where $\hat d_k$ is the estimated horizontal UAV--UGV offset (the first term keeps the target
within the footprint), $\hat\kappa_k$ is the anticipated path curvature, and
$\beta_\kappa\hat\kappa_k$ raises altitude into turns where the required lookahead
$d_{\mathrm{look}}(\kappa)$ --- and hence the coverage floor $\eta\!\gtrsim\!1$
\eqref{eq:eta} --- grows; $z_{\mathrm{m}}$ is a fixed margin that keeps the forward lookahead in
view on straights. ``Climb into turns to widen the footprint'' is thus a
derived consequence of the coverage constraint, not a hand-coded rule.

\subsection{Offline robust-invariant-set baseline}
\label{sec:mrpi}
The ancillary law is $\mathbf{u}_k=\bar{\mathbf{u}}_k-K\mathbf{e}_k$ with tracking
error $\mathbf{e}_k=\mathbf{p}_k-\bar{\mathbf{p}}_k$; substituting into
\eqref{eq:uav_disc},
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{e}_{k+1}=\underbrace{(A_d-B_dK)}_{=:A_K}\mathbf{e}_k+\mathbf{w}_k,
\qquad \mathbf{w}_k\in\mathbb{W}.
\label{eq:error_dynamics}
\end{equation}
For $A_K$ Schur and $\mathbb{W}$ compact, the minimal RPI set is outer-approximated \emph{offline}
by a truncated Minkowski series and the constraints tightened by the Pontryagin difference
\cite{mRPI},
\begin{equation}
\Omega=\textstyle\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}A_K^i\mathbb{W}\supseteq\Omega_\infty,\quad
\bar{\mathbb{X}}=\mathbb{X}\ominus\Omega,\ \ \bar{\mathbb{U}}=\mathbb{U}\ominus K\Omega .
\label{eq:mrpi}
\end{equation}
We use this only as a \emph{design-time baseline}: $\Omega$ is a certified robust bound for the
idealized system and seeds the ellipsoidal shape matrix $P_0$ below, but it is \emph{not}
propagated online. The next subsection states plainly what the deployed controller uses instead
and what guarantee is traded away.

\subsection{Online adaptive ellipsoidal tube}
Propagating the polytope $\Omega$ online --- vertex/support-function updates and a
Pontryagin difference at every $20$\,Hz ancillary step --- is impractical on the UAV's
embedded companion processor. Rather than pay that overhead, the ancillary controller uses
an \emph{ellipsoidal} invariant-set surrogate
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}(\alpha_k)=\{\mathbf{e}: \mathbf{e}^T P_0\,\mathbf{e}\le\alpha_k^2\},
\label{eq:ellipsoid}
\end{equation}
with a shape matrix $P_0$ (loaded from the offline design, else a Schur-consistent
fallback) and a radius that adapts to the EKF horizontal covariance trace
$\tr\Sigma_{xy}=\Sigma_{xx}+\Sigma_{yy}$:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_k=\alpha_{\min}+(\alpha_{\max}-\alpha_{\min})\,
\mathrm{clip}\!\Big(\tfrac{\tr\Sigma_{xy}-\tr_{\min}}{\tr_{\max}-\tr_{\min}},0,1\Big),
\label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
($\alpha_{\min}=0.5,\ \alpha_{\max}=2$). The tube \emph{breathes}: it tightens
when the estimate is sharp and loosens when perception degrades. We stress that
\eqref{eq:alpha} scales the \emph{invariant-set membership/trigger radius} used
online; it is \emph{not} accompanied by an online re-derivation of
\eqref{eq:mrpi} nor by re-tightening $\bar{\mathbb{X}},\bar{\mathbb{U}}$. A
homothetic tube with a matching recursive-feasibility argument is future work
(Section~\ref{sec:theory}).
Accordingly, $\alpha_k$ is a heuristic monitor/trigger radius, not a certified
invariant radius. The certified objects are the \emph{offline} mRPI set $\Omega$
\eqref{eq:mrpi} and the $A_K$-Schur ISS bound of Sec.~\ref{sec:theory} (which rests
on the contraction $A_K$ and bounded $\mathbb{W}$, not on $\alpha_k$). The choice
$\alpha_{\min}<1$ tunes \emph{trigger sensitivity} (how eagerly a replan fires),
not invariance; letting the membership radius drop below the nominal invariant size
therefore does not weaken those certified guarantees or contradict the offline mRPI
bound.

\subsection{Perception-driven event trigger}
\label{sec:trigger}
The ancillary node runs at $20$\,Hz but requests a nominal replan only when one of
four prioritized conditions fires (implemented literally as follows).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(1) Tube exit (critical, no lockout):} fire iff
$\mathbf{e}_k^T P_0\mathbf{e}_k>\alpha_k^2$, i.e.\ the state left the adaptive
ellipsoid \eqref{eq:ellipsoid}.
\item \textbf{(2) Covariance spike:} $\tr\Sigma_{xy}(t)>\tau_{\mathrm{cov}}$ and a
sudden ($>\!1.5\times$) rise over $\sim0.5$\,s (e.g.\ ArUco occlusion).
\item \textbf{(3) Curvature anticipation:} the discrete curvature of the UGV predicted path,
$\hat\kappa=\max_i |\Delta\theta_i|/\Delta s_i$ --- the wrapped heading change $\Delta\theta_i$
between consecutive predicted waypoints over their spacing $\Delta s_i$, maximized over the next
few steps --- exceeds $\tau_\kappa=0.4$\,rad/m, so the UAV climbs before the turn (raising the
coverage floor of Sec.~\ref{sec:coverage}).
\item \textbf{(4) Temporal drift:} elevated $\tr\Sigma_{xy}$ with time since last
replan exceeding $T_{\min}$.
\end{itemize}
Conditions (2)--(4) carry a $1$\,s time-lockout (the chattering guard);
condition (1) has none. Between triggers the ancillary law regulates the error
via \eqref{eq:error_dynamics}, and the previous nominal sequence is shifted
forward.

We note that a purely fractional trigger on the \emph{polytopic} tube,
$\max(H_\Omega\mathbf{e}_k-\sigma h_\Omega)>0$ with contracted set
$\Omega_\sigma$, is the natural formulation for the offline mRPI $\Omega$ and
would furnish the tunable computation--accuracy parameter $\sigma$; the
implementation instead uses the ellipsoidal test above, so we present the
$\sigma$-polytopic trigger as future work rather than as an implemented result.


\subsection{Nominal optimization actually solved}
At each trigger the planner solves \eqref{eq:Ja} over the single integrator
\eqref{eq:uav_disc} with velocity bounds $v_x,v_y\in[-1.5,1.5]$,
$v_z\in[-1,1]$\,m/s, warm-started from the previous solution (SLSQP,
$\text{maxiter}=15$). Altitude limits $z\in[z_{\min},z_{\max}]$ are shaped by the
cost; a hard terminal set $\mathbb{X}_f$ and the DARE terminal cost of the
idealized formulation below are \emph{not} enforced online.

\section{Theoretical Properties and Implementation Status}
\label{sec:theory}

We separate what is proven, what is inherited under assumptions, and what is only
empirical.

\textbf{Idealized recursive feasibility.} For the error system
\eqref{eq:error_dynamics} with $\Omega$ a true RPI set and tightened constraints
$\bar{\mathbb{X}},\bar{\mathbb{U}}$, a nominal MPC with a control-invariant
terminal set $\mathbb{X}_f$ and DARE terminal cost is recursively feasible and the
true state satisfies $\mathbf{x}_k\in\bar{\mathbb{X}}\oplus\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{X}$
\cite{mayne2005robust,mRPI}. This holds for the \emph{idealized} design; the code
uses the ellipsoidal surrogate \eqref{eq:ellipsoid} and does not enforce
tightening or a terminal set, so online recursive feasibility is \emph{assumed,
not verified}.

\textbf{Bounded inter-trigger error (ISS).} With $A_K$ Schur and
$\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{W}$ bounded, \eqref{eq:error_dynamics} is input-to-state
stable. For the implemented contraction $A_K=0.6\,I_3$ this is immediate:
$\lVert\mathbf{e}_{k+1}\rVert\le 0.6\lVert\mathbf{e}_k\rVert+\sup\lVert\mathbf{w}\rVert$,
hence $\limsup_k\lVert\mathbf{e}_k\rVert\le\sup\lVert\mathbf{w}\rVert/0.4$. Thus,
between triggers, the error stays bounded and the ellipsoid membership is a
meaningful monitor.

\textbf{Belief-MPC stability.} Adding $\lambda\tr\Sigma_k$ and $c_{\mathrm{fov}}$
to the stage cost \eqref{eq:Jg} makes it nonconvex and state-dependent; the
standard terminal-cost machinery does not directly apply and we do not claim a
formal stability proof for the UGV controller.

\textbf{Stochastic/bounded mismatch.} The mRPI machinery needs a bounded
$\mathbb{W}$, whereas the EKF noise is Gaussian. The offline computation truncates
$\mathbf{v}_k$ to a box without a stated confidence level, and the online
$\Sigma$ is never mapped to a hard bound. A probabilistic/stochastic-tube
treatment is future work.

\textbf{Limitations and honest scope.} The study is in simulation only; the UAV is
a point-mass single integrator; the tube adaptivity is in the trigger/monitor
radius, not a re-derived invariant set; the mRPI is offline and not wired into the
online loop; and no online recursive-feasibility certificate is enforced. Most
importantly, \emph{the isolated observability benefit of perception-awareness is
not separable on this platform}, for two reasons detailed in Sec.~\ref{sec:results}:
(a) because the shared downward camera keeps the target near the optical center (mean
UAV--UGV offset $0.06$--$0.12$\,m), the estimation covariance is dominated by the
altitude-dependent measurement noise and carries no separable tracking-difficulty
signal; and (b) the low-altitude/high-speed corner that would stress the coverage
margin is non-reproducibly unstable. We stress that this residual non-reproducibility
is confined to that binding corner: in the operating regime the closed loop \emph{is}
reproducible once run headless (identical-configuration trajectories agree to
$\approx\!\RESforkHL$\,m; with the Gazebo GUI, rendering contention with the ROS\,2
executor forks them by $\approx\!\RESforkGUI$\,m). Establishing the isolated
observability benefit is future work requiring the ground vehicle's navigation to be
decoupled from the tracking camera (onboard odometry or a second exteroceptive sensor).

\section{Simulation Study}
\label{sec:results}

\textbf{Setup.} The system runs in Gazebo with ROS\,2: a TurtleBot3-class UGV with
an ArUco marker, an X500-class UAV with a downward camera, an OpenCV lane/marker
pipeline, the 5-state EKF, the belief-space UGV MPC, and the UAV
planner/ancillary pair. Per-step telemetry (pose, ground truth, $\tr\Sigma$, FoV
distance, ArUco visibility, view quality, replan events, solve times) is logged
by a common logger identical across configurations.

\textbf{Reproducibility (a prerequisite, and a methodological finding).} Closed-loop
active-perception study requires reproducible runs. We found the ROS\,2/Gazebo pipeline is
\emph{not} reproducible when run with the GUI: identical-configuration ground-truth
trajectories fork by $\approx\!\RESforkGUI$\,m and ArUco visibility swings between $0$ and
$100\%$, because the GUI rendering thread contends with the ROS\,2 executor and perturbs the
closed perception--control--physics loop. Running \emph{headless} (server-only) removes this
contention: identical-configuration trajectories then agree to $\approx\!\RESforkHL$\,m and
ArUco visibility is consistently $100\%$. All results below are headless. We report this
because it is an often-unstated prerequisite for reproducible closed-loop SITL evaluation.

\textbf{Co-design: event-triggering makes the belief-space planner affordable.} The belief-space
planner propagates the EKF covariance through its horizon and is comparatively expensive per
solve. Continuous replanning (C1) spends a large, sustained CPU budget; the perception-driven
event trigger (C2) invokes the \emph{same} planner only when a covariance/curvature/drift
condition fires. On the nominal track (headless, $R{=}3$), C2 reduces planner invocations
$\approx\!\RESsolveRed\times$ ($\RESsolvesCone\!\to\!\RESsolvesCthree$) and total planner CPU
$\approx\!\REScpuRed\times$ ($\REScpuCone\!\to\!\REScpuCthree$\,ms per $45$\,s run;
Fig.~\ref{fig:codesign}), at matched estimation quality (steady-state $\tr\Sigma=\REScov$ and
$100\%$ ArUco visibility for both). Per-solve time is \emph{higher} for C2
($\RESmsCthree$ vs.\ $\RESmsCone$\,ms), consistent with a warm-start disparity --- C1 re-solves
every $50$\,ms from a near-identical previous solution, whereas each C2 solve starts from a
larger state mismatch --- but the $\approx\!\RESsolveRed\times$ reduction in solve count
dominates, giving the net CPU saving. This is the co-design point: event-triggering makes the
heavier perception-aware planner affordable on a constrained companion computer. We are precise
about what this does and does not show: the saving is attributable to event-triggering
\emph{per se} (any state-dependent trigger vs.\ continuous replanning), a well-established
effect; isolating the contribution of the \emph{perception-specific} conditions (covariance
spike, curvature) against a plain error- or time-triggered baseline is a separate ablation. Our
findings above --- estimation quality that is insensitive to the trigger where the FoV does not
bind --- suggest that ablation would show near-parity in the reproducible regime, consistent
with the coupling of Sec.~\ref{sec:coverage}; we therefore defer it to future work rather than
claim a perception-trigger advantage we cannot presently isolate.

\textbf{What event-triggering costs.} It is not free: applying the previous nominal trajectory
between triggers introduces small control discontinuities at trigger instants. We measure
$\approx\!20\times$ higher high-frequency trajectory content for C2 than C1 (RMS of the second
position difference). The mean UAV--UGV offset is nonetheless smaller for C2
($\RESoffCthree$ vs.\ $\RESoffCone$\,m); we do \emph{not} attribute this to event-triggering
(the arms differ in planner cadence) and report it only for completeness. The honest summary is
a standard event-triggered tradeoff: a large compute saving for a small loss of inter-trigger
trajectory smoothness, at matched estimation quality.

\textbf{The coverage--resolution altitude tension.} With the GSD model enabled, we sweep
altitude across three tracks of increasing curvature (headless; low-altitude cells are the
median of $R{=}3$ reps). Two costs move oppositely with altitude (Fig.~\ref{fig:envelope}):
estimation uncertainty $\tr\Sigma$ \emph{decreases} as altitude decreases ($0.0094$ at
$z{=}6$\,m to $0.0053$ at $z{=}2$\,m --- the $R\propto z^2$ resolution gain, and
track-independent), while the UGV steering effort needed to keep the path segmented
\emph{increases} as altitude decreases, and increases with path curvature. Hence the
perception-constrained policy: fly as low as segmentation allows, raising altitude on
anticipated curvature where the navigation floor rises. In SITL the active-altitude policy
actuates altitude closed-loop, the UAV spanning $z\in[2.5,7]$\,m as the curvature-driven
setpoint varies (versus a fixed altitude in the baseline). We note that a clean anticipatory
\emph{lead} of the achieved altitude over the realized curvature is not isolable in SITL: the
policy's prediction horizon is short and the closed-loop tracking adds lag, so the achieved
altitude is roughly coincident with the turn; the anticipation property itself
(Prop.~3) is established analytically and in the exact numerical model, not claimed as an SITL
result.

\textbf{Scope, stated as findings.} Two limits are findings, not hidden. \emph{(a) The isolated
observability benefit is not separable on this platform.} Because the single downward camera
serves both navigation and estimation, and the tracker holds the target near the optical
centre, $\tr\Sigma$ is dominated by the altitude-dependent measurement noise --- it is
essentially identical across tracks at a given altitude --- so it carries no separable
tracking-difficulty signal. \emph{(b) The binding corner is non-reproducibly unstable.} At
$z{=}2$\,m and UGV speed $1.0$\,m/s --- exactly where the coverage constraint binds hardest ---
identical configurations yield ArUco visibility from $35\%$ to $100\%$, so no crisp feasibility
boundary can be reported there. Establishing the isolated observability benefit would require
decoupling the ground vehicle's navigation from the tracking camera (onboard odometry or a
second sensor); we leave it as future work.

\section{CONCLUSIONS}

We studied aerial--ground cooperative tracking where a single downward UAV camera is the
ground vehicle's sole exteroceptive sensor, serving both its navigation reference and its state
estimate, in a genuinely-controlled, headless-reproducible ROS\,2/Gazebo SITL system. We
formalized the resulting coverage--resolution altitude tension, derived the
perception-constrained altitude policy (fly as low as segmentation allows, climbing into
turns), and showed a perception-driven event trigger is the computational enabler of the
belief-space planner --- $\approx\!\RESsolveRed\times$ fewer solves and $\approx\!\REScpuRed\times$
less planner CPU at matched estimation quality, for a small loss of inter-trigger trajectory
smoothness. We were explicit that the isolated observability benefit is not separable on this
platform (the shared camera couples navigation and estimation) and that the binding
low-altitude/high-speed corner is non-reproducibly unstable --- both stated as findings, and
both pointing to the same architectural cause. Future work: decouple the ground vehicle's
navigation from the tracking camera (onboard odometry or a second sensor) to isolate the
observability benefit; a full multicopter model for hardware-faithful altitude dynamics; and,
on the theory side, a homothetic tube with recursive-feasibility guarantees.
