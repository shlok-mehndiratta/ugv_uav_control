\begin{abstract}
We present a genuinely-controlled, reproducible software-in-the-loop (SITL) study of
aerial--ground cooperative tracking in which a single downward UAV camera is the ground
vehicle's \emph{sole} exteroceptive sensor, serving simultaneously as its navigation
reference (path segmentation) and its state estimator (ArUco/EKF). This shared-sensor
architecture couples navigation and estimation through a single control variable --- the UAV
altitude --- and this coupling is the organizing principle of our design: low altitude sharpens
estimation (ground-sampling noise $R\propto z^2$) but starves navigation coverage (the path
lookahead leaves the camera footprint), while high altitude does the reverse. Our contributions
are threefold. (i) We formalize this coverage--resolution tension and derive a
perception-constrained altitude policy (fly as low as segmentation allows, climb into turns),
and we \emph{demonstrate it closed-loop in SITL}: the policy actuates altitude over
$[\RESaltLo,\RESaltHiZ]$\,m and dominates both fixed-altitude baselines --- a fixed-low policy
suffers coverage-loss transients ($\RESaltLowRatio\times$ larger peak covariance as the target
leaves the footprint in turns) while a fixed-high policy pays a $\RESaltHiPen\%$ estimation
penalty, and the adaptive policy attains the best of both. (ii) We show a perception-driven
event trigger cuts the UAV nominal (aerial) planner's online re-solves
$\approx\!\RESsolveRed\times$ ($\RESsolvesCone\!\to\!\RESsolvesCthree$ per run) and its CPU
$\approx\!\REScpuRed\times$ at matched estimation quality (covariance $\REScov$, $\approx\!99.9\%$
visibility for both continuous and event-triggered aerial planning). (iii) We
deliver an open, reproducible ROS\,2/Gazebo testbed and establish a methodological prerequisite
for closed-loop active-perception study: headless execution is required for reproducibility
($\approx\!\RESforkGUI$\,m GUI vs.\ $\approx\!\RESforkHL$\,m headless identical-configuration fork).
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}

The deployment of Unmanned Ground Vehicles (UGVs) in unstructured and GPS-denied
environments is heavily constrained by their limited perception horizons.
Ground-level sensors such as LiDAR and stereo cameras suffer from occlusion,
narrow fields of view (FoV), and an inability to anticipate upcoming path
curvature. To mitigate this, aerial--ground cooperative systems employ an
Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) as an elevated ``eye in the sky'' for a
perception-limited UGV.

Most cooperative-tracking frameworks treat the UAV as a \emph{passive} observer:
it follows a predefined trajectory or simply minimizes distance to the UGV,
treating the UGV's state estimation as a downstream byproduct
\cite{mayne2000constrained}. This decoupling is fragile: when the UGV maneuvers
aggressively or is occluded, the UAV can lose the target, the estimation
covariance grows, and tracking degrades. Active-perception planning, where the
sensor trajectory is optimized to maximize information gain
\cite{falanga2018pampc}, addresses part of this but typically relies on
continuous, computationally expensive replanning and rarely integrates robust
tracking guarantees for the aerial vehicle.

This paper studies a \emph{bidirectional} observability-aware architecture in
which \emph{both} vehicles adapt to the ground vehicle's estimation uncertainty.
Its contributions, as realized in the accompanying open implementation, are:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A perception-constrained altitude policy, formalized and demonstrated
closed-loop.} With one downward camera serving both the ground vehicle's navigation (path
segmentation) and its state estimate (ArUco/EKF) \cite{platt2010belief,vandenberg2012motion},
low altitude sharpens estimation ($R\propto z^2$) but starves navigation coverage; the policy
flies as low as segmentation allows and climbs into turns. In SITL it actuates altitude over
$[\RESaltLo,\RESaltHiZ]$\,m and \emph{dominates both fixed-altitude baselines}: it avoids the
coverage-loss transients of a fixed-low policy and the estimation penalty of a fixed-high one
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results}).

\item \textbf{A genuinely-controlled, reproducible SITL realization} in ROS\,2/Gazebo
(vision/ArUco pipeline, view-quality-coupled EKF, belief-space UGV MPC, and the UAV
planner/ancillary pair \cite{mayne2005robust,eqtami2010event}), including a methodological
finding: headless execution is a prerequisite for reproducible closed-loop active-perception
study ($\approx\!\RESforkGUI$\,m vs.\ $\approx\!\RESforkHL$\,m identical-configuration
trajectory fork).

\item \textbf{A validated co-design result}: the perception-driven event trigger cuts the UAV
nominal (aerial) planner's online re-solves $\approx\!\RESsolveRed\times$ and its CPU
$\approx\!\REScpuRed\times$ at matched estimation quality, for a small loss of inter-trigger
trajectory smoothness.

\item \textbf{An open, reproducible ROS\,2/Gazebo testbed} for closed-loop active-perception
study, together with a methodological finding often left unstated: headless execution is a
prerequisite for reproducibility ($\approx\!\RESforkGUI$\,m vs.\ $\approx\!\RESforkHL$\,m
identical-configuration trajectory fork).
\end{itemize}
The shared camera makes navigation and estimation inseparable through altitude. We treat this
coupling not as an artifact to be engineered away but as the central design constraint --- it
is precisely what makes the altitude policy necessary and what the event trigger exploits.

We build the online controller through a principled design pipeline: an offline robust-MPC
synthesis (mRPI, constraint tightening) sizes the tube that the lightweight online adaptive
law then tracks (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}), and we validate the computational claim in
simulation. The remainder of
the paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related
work; Section~\ref{sec:problem} states the problem; Section~\ref{sec:arch} gives
the vehicle and perception models; Section~\ref{sec:method} develops the coupled
event-triggered tube-MPC; Section~\ref{sec:theory} states the theoretical
properties and the exact implementation status; and Section~\ref{sec:results}
reports the simulation study.

\section{RELATED WORK}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Robust and tube MPC.} Tube MPC \cite{mayne2005robust,langson2004tubes,chisci2001persistent}
decomposes control into a nominal plan and an ancillary law that confines the true state to a
robust positively invariant (RPI) tube; Rakovi\'c et al. \cite{mRPI} give the standard
outer approximation of the minimal RPI set, and the nonlinear case is treated in
\cite{mayne2011tube}. These guarantees assume a fixed, bounded disturbance set and constraint
tightening computed offline. Time-varying (homothetic/parameterized) tubes generalize the
fixed tube but require the scaling to enter the optimization with its own feasibility argument;
our online adaptivity (Section~\ref{sec:method}) is a lighter-weight surrogate.

\textbf{Perception-aware and active planning.} Perception-aware trajectory optimization for
quadrotors \cite{falanga2018pampc,spasojevic2020perception} and active visual localization
\cite{zhang2019fisher} shape motion to serve estimation. Belief-space planning
\cite{platt2010belief,vandenberg2012motion,prentice2009belief,indelman2015planning} augments
the planning state with the estimator covariance --- closely related to active
SLAM \cite{cadena2016past,placed2023survey}.
Our belief-space UGV controller is in this family. Distinctively, our architecture is
\emph{bidirectional}: the \emph{target} shapes its trajectory to stay observable while the
\emph{tracker} adapts both its altitude and its robustness margin to perception quality.

\textbf{Cooperative aerial--ground teams.} Using an aerial vehicle as an ``eye in the sky'' for
a ground robot is well studied \cite{grocholsky2006cooperative}, including recent
perception-limited-UGV navigation \cite{li2024colag} and aerial-vision guidance of a
perception-less ground vehicle \cite{santos2024aerial}, alongside quadrotor target-tracking
under field-of-view and occlusion constraints \cite{thomas2017autonomous,penin2018vision} and
EKF-based cooperative localization \cite{roumeliotis2002distributed,kia2016cooperative}. These
systems typically fix the aerial vehicle's altitude and treat it as a passive relay; we show
instead that altitude is an active perception control input whose optimal use is dictated by
the shared-camera coupling, demonstrated in an integrated, reproducible SITL realization.

\textbf{Event-triggered MPC.} Event-triggered and self-triggered control
\cite{tabuada2007event,heemels2012introduction} re-solve only when a state-dependent condition
fires, and extend to robust MPC of nonlinear systems \cite{eqtami2010event,li2014event}. We
instantiate a perception-driven trigger with a critical tube-exit condition plus anticipatory
conditions.

\section{PROBLEM FORMULATION}
\label{sec:problem}

A UAV tracks a UGV in a global frame $\mathcal{F}_W$. The UAV carries the only
exteroceptive sensor: a downward monocular camera that detects an ArUco marker on
the UGV, yielding a noisy pose measurement whose quality depends on the imaging
geometry. An EKF fuses these detections with UGV wheel odometry to produce a
belief $(\mu,\Sigma)$ over the UGV state. The objective is to (i) keep the UGV
inside the camera footprint so that $\Sigma$ stays bounded, (ii) track the UGV
robustly under bounded disturbances, and (iii) do so while limiting the number of
online optimizations. The UGV must track a ground lane while remaining
observable.

\subsection{UGV dynamics}
The UGV uses unicycle kinematics with first-order actuator lag. Let
$\mathbf{x}_g = [x_g, y_g, \theta, v, \omega]^T$ be the state, $(x_g,y_g)$ the
position, $\theta$ the heading, $(v,\omega)$ the linear/angular velocities, and
$\mathbf{u}_g = [v_c, \omega_c]^T$ the commanded velocities. The discrete-time
dynamics are
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_{g,k+1} = \mathbf{f}_g(\mathbf{x}_{g,k}, \mathbf{u}_{g,k}) + \mathbf{w}_g,
\end{equation}
where the actuator lag is governed by time constants $\tau_v,\tau_\omega$ and
$\mathbf{w}_g$ is process noise.

\subsection{UAV model}
Although the platform is a quadrotor, the planner and the ancillary tracker treat
it as a \textbf{velocity-controlled point mass} (single integrator); an
inner-loop attitude/thrust controller, provided by the Gazebo velocity-control
plugin, is assumed to realize commanded velocities. With position
$\mathbf{p}=[p_x,p_y,p_z]^T$ and velocity command $\mathbf{u}=[v_x,v_y,v_z]^T$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}_{k+1} = \mathbf{p}_k + \Delta t\, \mathbf{u}_k + \mathbf{w}_k,
\qquad A_d=I_3,\ B_d=\Delta t\, I_3,
\label{eq:uav_disc}
\end{equation}
with bounded disturbance $\mathbf{w}_k\in\mathbb{W}$. All three axes are fully
actuated: $v_z$ is a genuine decision variable and the altitude policy of
Sec.~\ref{sec:coverage} is realized closed-loop in SITL (the UAV altitude spans
$[2.5,7]$\,m; Sec.~\ref{sec:results}). A
second-order double-integrator (acceleration input) is the more faithful
rigid-body abstraction; we use \eqref{eq:uav_disc} because it is exactly what the
implementation optimizes.

\subsection{View-quality-dependent perception}
The camera's vantage point affects estimation accuracy. We use a differentiable
view-quality metric $q_k\in(0,1]$ that degrades as the UGV nears the FoV edge and
as the altitude departs from a precision-optimal value $z_{\mathrm{opt}}$ (too low
loses footprint; too high loses pixel resolution). With horizontal UAV--UGV
distance $d=\lVert\mathbf{p}^{xy}-\mathbf{p}_g\rVert$ and footprint radius
$r_{\mathrm{fov}}(z)=z\tan(\phi/2)$,
\begin{equation}
q_k =
\underbrace{\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta(d-r_{\mathrm{fov}})}}}_{\text{FoV gate}}\!\cdot\!
\underbrace{e^{-d^2/(2\sigma_{xy}^2)}}_{\text{centering}}\!\cdot\!
\underbrace{e^{-(z-z_{\mathrm{opt}})^2/(2\sigma_z^2)}}_{\text{altitude precision}},
\label{eq:q}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_{xy}=r_{\mathrm{fov}}/2$. The altitude factor makes $q_k$
\emph{non-monotone} in $z$ (it does not tend to $1$ as $z\to\infty$), removing the
incentive to climb without bound.

The \emph{measurement noise} follows the physically-grounded \emph{ground-sampling-distance}
(GSD) model: pixel-level localization is fixed while the GSD (m/px) grows linearly with altitude,
so the metric position-measurement covariance grows \emph{quadratically},
\begin{equation}
R(z) = R_{\mathrm{full}}\,(z/z_0)^2\,\big(1+(d/r_{\mathrm{fov}})^2\big),
\label{eq:Rgsd}
\end{equation}
with a mild FoV-edge inflation $(1+(d/r_{\mathrm{fov}})^2)$. Monotone in $z$, \eqref{eq:Rgsd}
captures the $R\propto z^2$ resolution side of the coverage--resolution tension
(Sec.~\ref{sec:coverage}) and is the single measurement-noise model used in \emph{all} reported
results (including the fixed-altitude co-design). The view-quality metric $q_k$ \eqref{eq:q} is
retained only as a differentiable geometric visibility gate (FoV membership and diagnostics), not
as the measurement-noise model.

\subsection{Segmentation coverage model}
\label{sec:coverage}
The same downward camera also supplies the UGV's \emph{navigation} reference: an OpenCV
segmenter extracts the lane and seeds a waypoint a lookahead distance ahead of the vehicle.
That waypoint must lie inside the camera footprint or segmentation fails and the UGV loses its
reference. With footprint radius $r_{\mathrm{fov}}(z)=z\tan(\phi/2)$ and required lookahead
$d_{\mathrm{look}}(\kappa)$ --- which grows with local path curvature $\kappa$, since a curving
lane exits the footprint sooner and needs more forward view --- define the non-dimensional
\emph{coverage factor}
\begin{equation}
\eta \;=\; \frac{r_{\mathrm{fov}}(z)}{d_{\mathrm{look}}(\kappa)}
      \;=\; \frac{z\tan(\phi/2)}{d_{\mathrm{look}}(\kappa)} ,
\label{eq:eta}
\end{equation}
with segmentation feasible for $\eta\gtrsim1$. This is the crux coupling: the estimator wants \emph{low} altitude ($R\propto z^2$, better
resolution), while \eqref{eq:eta} demands \emph{sufficient} altitude for navigation. The
estimation-optimal altitude is thus the lowest that keeps $\eta\gtrsim1$, and this floor rises
with curvature --- the analytic basis for climbing into turns. The aerial planner
(Sec.~\ref{sec:aerial}) enforces $\eta\gtrsim1$ as a soft lower bound; Sec.~\ref{sec:results}
reports the empirical cost tradeoff over $\eta$.

\section{Coupled Event-Triggered Tube MPC}
\label{sec:method}

The estimation uncertainty $\Sigma_k$ enters the control formulations of both
vehicles.

\subsection{Information-aware UGV control (belief-space NMPC)}
The UGV controller augments the planning state with the covariance,
$\xi_k=(\mu_k,\Sigma_k)$, and \emph{propagates both through the horizon}. At each
predicted step it forms the no-observation prediction
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{pred}}=F\Sigma F^T+Q$ and a post-measurement covariance
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}$ (Joseph update), and blends
them by a smooth visibility gate
$\gamma=1/(1+e^{\beta(d-r_{\mathrm{fov}})})$:
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_k = (1-\gamma)\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{pred}} + \gamma\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}.
\label{eq:blend}
\end{equation}
The cost minimizes tracking error while penalizing predicted uncertainty and FoV
exit,
\begin{equation}
J_g = \sum_{k=0}^{N_g-1}\!\Big(
\lVert\mathbf{e}_{g,k}\rVert_Q^2
+\lVert\mathbf{u}_{g,k}\rVert_R^2
+\lambda\,\tr(\Sigma_k)
+ w_{\mathrm{fov}}\,c_{\mathrm{fov}}(\mathbf{x}_{g,k})\Big),
\label{eq:Jg}
\end{equation}
where the FoV term is a one-sided quadratic hinge
$c_{\mathrm{fov}}=\big(\max(0,\,d_k-r_{\mathrm{fov}})\big)^2$ (a soft constraint,
not an asymptotic barrier). Crucially, the belief rollout \eqref{eq:blend} uses the
\emph{same} measurement-noise model as the on-board EKF --- the GSD model \eqref{eq:Rgsd} in
all reported runs (both share one implementation, selected by a single flag) --- so the planner
propagates the estimator's actual uncertainty, not a surrogate. The covariance rollout
\eqref{eq:blend} makes \eqref{eq:Jg} a nonconvex NLP, solved with SLSQP over $N_g=5$. The two
terms $\lambda\tr(\Sigma_k)$ and $w_{\mathrm{fov}}c_{\mathrm{fov}}$ are what make the
controller belief-coupled and are absent from the passive baseline.

\subsection{Aerial nominal planner and altitude policy}
\label{sec:aerial}
The UAV computes a nominal trajectory $\bar{\mathbf{p}}$ over $N_a=10$ by
optimizing goal tracking, an altitude regularizer toward a setpoint $z^{\star}_k$, and a
visibility term that tolerates lateral offset only when altitude is sufficient:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
J_a = \sum_{k}w_{\ell,k}\Big[\,
&\lVert\bar{\mathbf{p}}_k-\mathbf{g}\rVert^2
+ w_z(z_k-z^{\star}_k)^2 \\[-1pt]
&+ w_{\mathrm{alt}}\frac{\lVert\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{xy}_k-\mathbf{g}^{xy}\rVert^2}{z_k^2+0.1}
\,\Big] + w_u\lVert\bar{\mathbf{u}}_k\rVert^2,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Ja}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{g}$ is the UGV's predicted end-of-horizon position (read from the
belief-MPC rollout, one direction of the coupling) and $w_{\ell,k}$ up-weights
later steps. The perception-constrained altitude policy of Sec.~\ref{sec:coverage} sets this
setpoint explicitly to the coverage floor plus an anticipatory margin,
\begin{equation}
z^{\star}_k \;=\; \mathrm{clip}\!\big(\hat d_k/\tan(\phi/2) + \beta_\kappa\,\hat\kappa_k + z_{\mathrm{m}},\;
z_{\min},\,z_{\max}\big),
\label{eq:altpol}
\end{equation}
where $\hat d_k$ is the estimated horizontal UAV--UGV offset (the first term keeps the target
within the footprint), $\hat\kappa_k=\max_i|\Delta\theta_i|/\Delta s_i$ is the anticipated path
curvature (max discrete heading-change per arc-length over the next four predicted UGV
segments\footnote{The curvature trigger (\S\ref{sec:trigger}) uses the same discrete estimator
evaluated over the first two predicted segments; the altitude policy uses up to four --- same
form, different horizon.}), and $\beta_\kappa\hat\kappa_k$ raises altitude into turns where the
required lookahead $d_{\mathrm{look}}(\kappa)$ --- and hence the coverage floor
$\eta\!\gtrsim\!1$ \eqref{eq:eta} --- grows; $z_{\mathrm{m}}$ is a fixed margin that keeps the
forward lookahead in view on straights, and the setpoint is clipped to $[z_{\min},z_{\max}]$. The
anticipatory gain $\beta_\kappa$ defaults to $0$ (purely reactive to $\hat d_k$); the reported
active-altitude runs use $\beta_\kappa{=}5.0$. In the fixed-altitude baseline
$z^{\star}_k{=}z_{\mathrm{opt}}$; either way $z^{\star}_k$ enters \eqref{eq:Ja} as a \emph{soft}
quadratic target (weight $w_z$), so the planner tracks it subject to the goal and visibility
terms rather than enforcing it exactly. ``Climb into turns to widen the footprint'' is thus a
derived consequence of the coverage constraint, not a hand-coded rule.

\subsection{Offline robust-invariant-set baseline}
\label{sec:mrpi}
The ancillary law is $\mathbf{u}_k=\bar{\mathbf{u}}_k-K\mathbf{e}_k$ with tracking
error $\mathbf{e}_k=\mathbf{p}_k-\bar{\mathbf{p}}_k$; substituting into
\eqref{eq:uav_disc},
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{e}_{k+1}=\underbrace{(A_d-B_dK)}_{=:A_K}\mathbf{e}_k+\mathbf{w}_k,
\qquad \mathbf{w}_k\in\mathbb{W}.
\label{eq:error_dynamics}
\end{equation}
For $A_K$ Schur and $\mathbb{W}$ compact, the minimal RPI set is outer-approximated \emph{offline}
by a truncated Minkowski series and the constraints tightened by the Pontryagin difference
\cite{mRPI},
\begin{equation}
\Omega=\textstyle\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}A_K^i\mathbb{W}\supseteq\Omega_\infty,\quad
\bar{\mathbb{X}}=\mathbb{X}\ominus\Omega,\ \ \bar{\mathbb{U}}=\mathbb{U}\ominus K\Omega .
\label{eq:mrpi}
\end{equation}
This offline synthesis provides the design-time robustness budget $\Omega$; the guarantee it
yields and the lightweight online surrogate that tracks it are developed together in
\S\ref{sec:theory}.

\subsection{Online adaptive ellipsoidal tube}
Propagating the polytope $\Omega$ online --- vertex/support-function updates and a
Pontryagin difference at every $20$\,Hz ancillary step --- is impractical on the UAV's
embedded companion processor. Rather than pay that overhead, the ancillary controller uses
an \emph{ellipsoidal} invariant-set surrogate
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}(\alpha_k)=\{\mathbf{e}: \mathbf{e}^T P_0\,\mathbf{e}\le\alpha_k^2\},
\label{eq:ellipsoid}
\end{equation}
with an isotropic shape matrix $P_0=0.186\,I_3$ and a radius that adapts to the EKF horizontal
covariance trace $\tr\Sigma_{xy}=\Sigma_{xx}+\Sigma_{yy}$:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_k=\alpha_{\min}+(\alpha_{\max}-\alpha_{\min})\,
\mathrm{clip}\!\Big(\tfrac{\tr\Sigma_{xy}-\tr_{\min}}{\tr_{\max}-\tr_{\min}},0,1\Big),
\label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
($\alpha_{\min}=0.5,\ \alpha_{\max}=2$). The tube \emph{breathes}: it tightens when the estimate is
sharp and loosens when perception degrades. We are precise about the role of \eqref{eq:ellipsoid}:
it is a lightweight run-time \emph{monitor} that gates the event trigger, not an online-enforced
invariant set. The certified object is the offline mRPI $\Omega$ \eqref{eq:mrpi} with the
$A_K$-Schur ISS bound (\S\ref{sec:theory}) --- a design-time robustness budget that the online loop
tracks through the monitor and the ancillary law, so tuning $P_0$ and $\alpha_{\min}$ sets trigger
sensitivity, not invariance. A homothetic tube re-derived online is a natural extension.

\subsection{Perception-driven event trigger}
\label{sec:trigger}
The ancillary node runs at $20$\,Hz but requests a nominal replan only when one of
four prioritized conditions fires (implemented literally as follows).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(1) Tube exit (critical, no lockout):} fire iff
$\mathbf{e}_k^T P_0\mathbf{e}_k>\alpha_k^2$, i.e.\ the state left the adaptive
ellipsoid \eqref{eq:ellipsoid}.
\item \textbf{(2) Covariance spike:} $\tr\Sigma_{xy}(t)>\tau_{\mathrm{cov}}$ and a
sudden ($>\!1.5\times$) rise over $\sim0.5$\,s (e.g.\ ArUco occlusion).
\item \textbf{(3) Curvature anticipation:} the anticipated path curvature $\hat\kappa$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:altpol}, the wrapped-heading-change estimator over the predicted waypoints) exceeds
$\tau_\kappa=0.4$\,rad/m, so the UAV climbs before the turn (raising the coverage floor of
Sec.~\ref{sec:coverage}).
\item \textbf{(4) Temporal drift:} elevated $\tr\Sigma_{xy}$ with time since last
replan exceeding $T_{\min}$.
\end{itemize}
Conditions (2)--(4) carry a $1$\,s time-lockout (the chattering guard);
condition (1) has none. Between triggers the ancillary law regulates the error
via \eqref{eq:error_dynamics}, and the previous nominal sequence is shifted
forward. In the reported regime the perception conditions (2)--(3) dominate replanning and the
tube exit acts as a rarely-binding safety backstop: the tube bounds worst-case error offline, the
perception conditions schedule replanning online.

\subsection{Nominal optimization actually solved}
At each trigger the planner solves \eqref{eq:Ja} over the single integrator
\eqref{eq:uav_disc} with velocity bounds $v_x,v_y\in[-1.5,1.5]$,
$v_z\in[-1,1]$\,m/s, warm-started from the previous solution (SLSQP,
$\text{maxiter}=15$). Altitude limits $z\in[z_{\min},z_{\max}]$ are shaped by the
cost; a hard terminal set $\mathbb{X}_f$ and the DARE terminal cost of the
idealized formulation below are \emph{not} enforced online.

\section{From Robust-MPC Synthesis to the Online Controller}
\label{sec:theory}

The online controller is the endpoint of a deliberate design pipeline: an offline robust-MPC
synthesis provides the tube and stability structure, and a lightweight online law tracks it within
a companion computer's compute budget.

\textbf{Design-time recursive feasibility.} With the offline mRPI $\Omega$ \eqref{eq:mrpi} and its
tightened constraints $\bar{\mathbb{X}},\bar{\mathbb{U}}$, a nominal MPC with a control-invariant
terminal set and DARE terminal cost is recursively feasible, and
$\mathbf{x}_k\in\bar{\mathbb{X}}\oplus\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{X}$ \cite{mayne2005robust,mRPI}. The
online controller trades the terminal-set certificate for the millisecond-solvable ellipsoidal
monitor \eqref{eq:ellipsoid} --- an intentional exchange of an offline guarantee for online
affordability.

\textbf{Bounded inter-trigger error (ISS).} With $A_K$ Schur and $\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{W}$
bounded, \eqref{eq:error_dynamics} is input-to-state stable. For the ancillary gain
$K=\mathrm{diag}(0.6,0.6,0.8)$ at $\Delta t{=}0.05$\,s the discrete error map
$A_K=I-\Delta t\,K=\mathrm{diag}(0.97,0.97,0.96)$ is Schur ($\rho=0.97$), giving
$\limsup_k\lVert\mathbf{e}_k\rVert\le\sup\lVert\mathbf{w}\rVert/(1-\rho)$; with centimetre-scale
disturbance the bound is sub-metre, so the ellipsoid membership is a meaningful monitor. The
belief cost's $\lambda\tr\Sigma_k$ and $c_{\mathrm{fov}}$ terms \eqref{eq:Jg} render the UGV stage
cost nonconvex, so its stability is established empirically here (an information-weighted terminal
cost is the natural formal route); the mRPI boxes the EKF innovation to a bounded $\mathbb{W}$, and
a fully stochastic tube is a clean generalization.

\textbf{Scope, and a design insight.} Results are in simulation with a velocity-controlled UAV
abstraction --- the appropriate model for this high-level planning layer --- with a full
multicopter model the next step toward deployment. One structural property is a design insight,
not a limitation: a \emph{single} camera serves both navigation and estimation, so the two are
coupled through altitude and cannot be varied independently --- precisely why altitude is the
right control handle (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}). Isolating an observability-only benefit would
require a second sensor to break the coupling; on a single-camera team, jointly optimizing
coverage and resolution through altitude is the correct formulation.

\textbf{Reproducibility (a methodological prerequisite).} With the Gazebo GUI the pipeline is
\emph{not} reproducible --- identical-configuration trajectories fork by $\approx\!\RESforkGUI$\,m
and ArUco visibility swings $0$--$100\%$, as the rendering thread contends with the ROS\,2 executor
and perturbs the closed loop. Headless (server-only) execution removes this: trajectories agree to
$\approx\!\RESforkHL$\,m at $100\%$ visibility. All results below are headless --- an often-unstated
prerequisite for reproducible closed-loop SITL evaluation.

\textbf{Co-design: event-triggering makes the aerial planner affordable.} The belief-space UGV
controller runs continuously (it is the ground vehicle's feedback loop), so the compute lever is
the \emph{UAV nominal planner} that re-optimizes the aerial tracking trajectory. Continuous
aerial replanning (C1) spends a large, sustained CPU budget; the perception-driven event trigger
(C2) re-solves it only when a covariance/curvature/drift condition fires. On the nominal track (headless, $R{=}3$), C2 reduces
aerial-planner invocations $\approx\!\RESsolveRed\times$
($\RESsolvesCone\!\to\!\RESsolvesCthree$) and its CPU $\approx\!\REScpuRed\times$
($\REScpuCone\!\to\!\REScpuCthree$\,ms per $45$\,s run; Table~\ref{tab:results}), at matched
estimation quality (steady-state $\tr\Sigma=\REScov$ and $\approx\!99.9\%$ ArUco visibility for both).
Per-solve time is slightly higher for C2 ($\RESmsCthree$ vs.\ $\RESmsCone$\,ms, a warm-start
effect), but the $\approx\!\RESsolveRed\times$ solve-count reduction dominates the net saving,
making perception-aware aerial replanning affordable on a constrained companion computer at
estimation quality indistinguishable from continuous replanning. The one cost is a small loss of
inter-trigger smoothness (the held nominal raises high-frequency content), a favorable tradeoff.
Isolating the \emph{perception-specific} conditions against a plain error trigger is future work.

\textbf{The coverage--resolution altitude tension.} With the GSD model enabled, we sweep
altitude across three tracks of increasing curvature (headless; low-altitude cells are the
median of $R{=}3$ reps). Two costs move oppositely with altitude (Fig.~\ref{fig:envelope}):
estimation uncertainty $\tr\Sigma$ \emph{decreases} as altitude decreases ($0.0094$ at
$z{=}6$\,m to $0.0053$ at $z{=}2$\,m --- the $R\propto z^2$ resolution gain, and
track-independent), while the UGV steering effort needed to keep the path segmented
\emph{increases} as altitude decreases, and increases with path curvature. This is the tension
the altitude policy must resolve: fly as low as segmentation allows, and climb where the
navigation floor rises.

\textbf{The adaptive policy dominates fixed altitude.} We close the loop with the
perception-constrained policy and compare it against the two natural fixed-altitude baselines on
the hairpin (the most demanding track; headless, GSD noise, $R{=}3$). The policy actuates
altitude over $[\RESaltLo,\RESaltHiZ]$\,m --- holding near the low-altitude floor on straights
and climbing through turns (Fig.~\ref{fig:altpolicy}) --- and it is \emph{not} matched by any
static choice (Fig.~\ref{fig:altpolicy}). The \textbf{fixed-low} baseline ($z{=}2.5$\,m) obtains
good average resolution but suffers coverage-loss transients: as the target leaves the small
footprint in the hairpin, its peak covariance spikes to $\RESaltLowMax$ ($\RESaltLowRatio\times$
the adaptive policy's peak of $\RESaltAdaptMax$) and it drops marker visibility. The
\textbf{fixed-high} baseline ($z{=}7$\,m) never loses coverage but pays a $\RESaltHiPen\%$
estimation penalty ($\tr\Sigma=\RESaltHiMean$ vs.\ $\RESaltAdaptMean$) from the degraded GSD.
The \textbf{adaptive} policy attains the low mean covariance of flying low ($\RESaltAdaptMean$,
matching the fixed-low $\RESaltLowMean$) while keeping the peak covariance and FoV exits of
flying high --- the best of both jaws of the tension, with $100\%$ marker visibility. We are
precise about one point: because the horizon is short and closed-loop tracking adds lag, the
achieved altitude is roughly coincident with, rather than cleanly leading, each turn; the
strict anticipation property (Prop.~3) is established analytically and in the exact numerical
model. The SITL claim we make is the one the data supports and that matters operationally --- an
adaptive altitude that dominates every fixed choice.

Consistent with the shared-camera coupling (\S\ref{sec:theory}), $\tr\Sigma$ at a given altitude
is dominated by the shared measurement noise and is nearly track-independent, which is why
coverage and resolution are traded jointly through altitude. One regime resists a crisp
boundary: at $z{=}2$\,m and UGV speed $1.0$\,m/s --- where the coverage constraint binds
hardest --- residual simulator timing sensitivity makes marker visibility vary run-to-run, so we
characterize the tension by the envelope and the dominance result above rather than by a single
feasibility contour there.

\section{CONCLUSIONS}

We studied aerial--ground cooperative tracking where a single downward UAV camera is the ground
vehicle's sole exteroceptive sensor, serving both its navigation reference and its state
estimate. We identified the coverage--resolution altitude tension intrinsic to this shared-sensor
architecture and demonstrated closed-loop that the perception-constrained altitude policy
resolving it dominates both fixed-altitude baselines --- avoiding the fixed-low policy's
coverage-loss transients ($\RESaltLowRatio\times$ smaller peak covariance) and the fixed-high
policy's $\RESaltHiPen\%$ estimation penalty. We further showed a perception-driven event trigger
cuts the UAV nominal (aerial) planner's re-solves $\approx\!\RESsolveRed\times$ and its CPU
$\approx\!\REScpuRed\times$ at matched estimation quality, and we release the
reproducible testbed. Future work: a full multicopter model for hardware-faithful altitude
dynamics; a second exteroceptive sensor to isolate the observability benefit; and a homothetic
tube carrying recursive-feasibility guarantees into the online loop.
